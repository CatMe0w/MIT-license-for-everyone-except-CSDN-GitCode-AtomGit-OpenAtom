# MIT License for Everyone Except CSDN/GitCode/AtomGit/OpenAtom

A protest license. Not recommended for actual use.

## Background

In 2016, artist Anish Kapoor acquired exclusive rights to Vantablack, the world's blackest pigment, preventing any other artist from using it. In response, artist Stuart Semple began selling his own pigments with a purchase condition: buyers must confirm they are not Anish Kapoor, not affiliated with him, and will not let the material reach him.

This repository applies the same idea to software. In 2024, CSDN's GitCode platform began systematically scraping GitHub repositories and republishing them, including entire organizations. It presented the mirrored content under the original maintainers' names and branding, creating the false impression that they had officially joined the platform. While open-source licenses permit forking and redistribution of code, they do not authorize impersonation of upstream maintainers. Later, when repositories contained code protesting this behavior, GitCode selectively excluded those branches.

In 2025, OpenAtom Foundation (开放原子开源基金会), a non-profit that positions itself as a neutral steward of open source, merged its own AtomGit platform into GitCode. A platform with a documented history of unauthorized mirroring now operates under the name of a public-interest foundation.

## Contents

- `LICENSE-permissive`: MIT-style grant with an entity exclusion
- `LICENSE-copyleft`: Same, plus a copyleft requirement

## Should I actually use this license?

Probably not.

Richard Stallman has argued that a program should not restrict what users do with the work it helps them accomplish. Freedom zero must be complete. Restricting use through a software license is ineffective, and worse than ineffective, because software authors should not have the power to dictate what users do. We agree.

But when an entity mirrors your code under your name without consent, and strips out the branches that protest their behavior, a symbolic gesture has its place. If you are looking for a real license, use any [OSI-approved license](https://opensource.org/licenses) such as MIT or GPL.
